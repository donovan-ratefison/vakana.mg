# 💡 Vakana: Possibilities

This file is a catalog of ways to use Vakana. Each line gives one concept. The concepts are organized by domain. Most concepts combine features that you know. Some concepts combine them in less obvious ways.

> 🔍 In the app, tap the **search icon** in the header to filter these sections by a word. Then tap a result to go to that section. After you scroll, a **Contents** pill in the corner brings you back to the list.

Use this file as a menu: use the entries that are useful to you and ignore the others. Each line names the Vakana features that it uses, so that you can find the patterns that you already use. The features are:

- thread
- auto-thread
- comment-reminder
- collection
- linked thread
- bead shape, mood and glyph
- section header
- markdown formatting
- calendar reminder

## 🪞 Personal reflection

- **Daily mood log**: Write one short entry each day. Use the bead color for the mood, and put each bead on a thread for the year.
- **Three good things**: Write three lines each evening. A single bead can show the tone of the day.
- **Morning pages**: Write a free entry each morning, with no bead and no thread. Use the search box to find old entries.
- **One sentence a day**: Write one line in each entry and put each entry on the year thread. The chain is the diary.
- **Energy log**: Write one entry for each part of the day. Use the bead mood for the energy level (forest = high, slate = neutral, charcoal = depleted). After some weeks, the pattern shows when your energy is highest.
- **Dream journal**: Write an entry when you wake up. Add a voice note for the parts that you cannot write. Set a comment-reminder for one week later that asks "what did this turn out to mean?".
- **End-of-week review**: Set a reminder for Sunday evening. Open the current `Week of …` auto-thread, which contains your starred entries as a chain. Then write a review entry that mentions the auto-thread.
- **Annual review**: Set a reminder for December. The 12 monthly auto-threads give the structure of the review of the year. You do not assemble a summary.
- **Bold the feeling**: Write `**so tired**` in a daily mood entry. The bold text shows in the bubble of the day auto-thread and in a *Storyboard* export of the chain.
- **Strikethrough the past view**: Write `~~I was sure~~ I'm starting to doubt` in a comment. The change of opinion stays visible when the comment-reminders fire later.

## 🌟 Weekly + monthly highlights

The star in the entry editor cycles `off → weekly → monthly → off`. A weekly highlight goes into a `Week of …` auto-thread, which is anchored on the ISO Monday. A monthly highlight also goes into a `Month` auto-thread. An auto-thread exists only while one entry or more references it. If you remove the star from the last entry, the auto-thread disappears. There are two tiers. The app uses no thresholds and no inference.

- **Friday star**: On Friday evening, look at the week and tap ★ on the entry that was important. The `Week of …` auto-thread becomes the chain of the important entries of the week.
- **Sunday-evening curation**: Look at the last 7 days and give the weekly star to the entries that you want to keep. Then use *Promote week to month* on the auto-week thread for the entries that are still important.
- **Two-tier sieve**: Do a fast first pass with weekly stars, which are easy to change. Do a slow second pass with *Promote week to month*. The time between the passes decides which entries go into the month. A threshold does not decide.
- **Lesson-of-the-week**: Give the weekly star to the one thing that you learned this week. Each `Week of …` auto-thread is then one chapter of lessons, and each month is one unit.
- **Decision-of-the-month**: Give the monthly star to each non-trivial decision entry when you write it. The `May 2026` auto-thread then shows the decisions of the month.
- **Person-of-the-week**: Give the star to an entry on a person-thread. The entry is then in the chain of that person and in the auto-thread of the week. No more work is necessary.
- **Monthly letter to a future self**: At the end of the month, the `May 2026` auto-thread is a letter about the month. It contains the bead chain, the photos, the voice notes and the mentions.
- **Travel highlights**: Write an entry for each day of a trip. Give the weekly star only to the best days. At the end of the trip, promote them, and the month auto-thread becomes the summary of the trip.
- **Hard-day monthly mark**: When a day is exceptionally good or bad, give the monthly star immediately and skip the weekly step. The `May 2026` auto-thread then shows the emotions of the month.
- **Auto-cull weak months**: A month with no monthly highlights does not get an auto-thread. The months that show in the Months section are the months that were important.
- **Weekly digest as Substack**: Open a `Week of …` auto-thread in the article composer, select the *Postcard* template, and share. This publishes your starred week.
- **Year-in-review skeleton**: In December, open the 12 monthly auto-threads and export each as `Editorial` HTML. Bind the exports into a book of the year. You do not write an annual summary.
- **Birthday-week roll-up**: Write a birthday entry on a person-thread and give it the weekly star. The `Week of …` auto-thread then shows the week around the birthday as a chain.
- **Postcard of a week**: Export a `Week of …` auto-thread as a *Postcard* HTML article. The export shows the week as a bead chain with the comments in the margin.
- **Self-ping promotion**: When a comment-reminder fires on an old entry that is still important, give the entry the monthly star. The entry then goes into the month auto-thread.
- **Practice-of-the-month**: Give the monthly star to entries on a per-practice thread (meditation, running). They go into the month auto-thread. The `May 2026` auto-thread then summarizes all practices, not one thread only.
- **Auto-thread anniversary**: One year later, open the `May 2025` and `May 2026` auto-threads and compare them. The difference shows the change during the year. A spreadsheet is not necessary.

## 🌱 Habits and practices

- **One thread per habit**: Make one thread for each habit (Run, Read, Meditate). Use the bead shape for the habit, so that you can read the chains quickly. A gap is a missed day.
- **Streak as a chain**: The visible length of the chain is the streak. A counter app is not necessary.
- **"Just show up" log**: Add a bead for one minute of practice too. The chain is then an accurate record of consistency, with no pressure to perform.
- **Recurring nudge → habit chain**: Use a daily recurring reminder together with a habit thread. Write an entry each time the reminder fires, and add its bead to the thread. The reminder keeps the cadence and the chain shows the result.
- **Bounded sprint**: For a 30/60/90-day practice, set a daily recurring reminder with a limit equal to the duration. The series deactivates itself when you acknowledge the last occurrence. No cleanup is necessary.
- **Sleep log**: Write an entry at bedtime. Set a comment-reminder for the time you wake up, and then grade the sleep.
- **Hydration / caffeine / medication**: Write an entry for each intake and use the bead pattern for the substance. The day auto-thread shows the pattern of the day. You can also set a daily recurring reminder for each dose.
- **Cold exposure / breathwork**: Write a short entry after each session. Use the mood color to record how the session felt.
- **Reading sessions**: Make a thread for each book and add a bead for each session. Put comments on the thread for the themes that you find.
- **Language learning**: Write an entry for each session and use a glyph for the language ("F", "JA"). A thread for each language shows the chain of practice. Add a daily recurring reminder if you want a streak.
- **Training cycle sections**: On a marathon-training thread, mark section beads "Base / Build / Peak / Taper". The chain then shows four phases and not one long streak.
- **Long-streak phases**: On a habit thread that continues for years, mark sections "Started / Plateau / Recovery / Renewal". The chain then shows the periods of doubt and return, and not only the count.

## 🩺 Health and body

- **Symptom journal**: Use the bead shape for the body system and the mood for the severity. A thread for each recurring symptom shows trends without a chart.
- **Period / cycle tracking**: Make a thread for each cycle and add a bead for each day. Use the mood color for the intensity and comments for context.
- **Pain log**: Use a bead shape for the body, the mood color for the pain level, and a glyph for the location ("L" lower back, "K" knee).
- **Therapy follow-through**: Write an entry after each session. Set a comment-reminder for each homework prompt, to fire in the middle of the week. A weekly recurring reminder with a limit equal to the remaining sessions can follow the appointment cadence.
- **Therapy theme promotion**: When a homework comment comes back in many sessions, promote it to a chained bead on a `Therapy themes` thread. The recurring action item becomes the lesson.
- **Recovery journal (post-surgery / illness)**: Write a daily entry and use the bead color to record how you feel. The chain shows the recovery. Set a daily recurring reminder with a limit equal to the expected recovery time.
- **Period / cycle tracking**: Set a recurring reminder every 28 days as a baseline. A thread for each cycle collects the details of each day.
- **Medication course**: Set a daily recurring reminder with a limit equal to the prescription duration. The series deactivates itself at the last dose.
- **Allergy / food diary**: Write an entry for each meal and use a bead glyph for the suspect ingredient. Search the entries later when symptoms appear.
- **Doctor visit notes**: Write the entry as a transcript of the appointment. Set comment-reminders for the follow-up actions.
- **Newborn feed / sleep log**: Give each entry its time-of-day. The day auto-thread shows the beads in clock order, so the chain shows the rhythm of the night.
- **Hour-stamped symptom flares**: Give the entry its time-of-day when a flare starts. Beads of the same day sort by the hour. The day auto-thread then shows if mornings or evenings are worse, without a chart.
- **Recovery phases as chapters**: On a recovery thread, mark section beads "Acute / Rehab / Maintenance". The chain shows when the condition changed, without a chart.
- **Pregnancy trimesters**: On a pregnancy thread, mark the first entry of each trimester as a section. The chain then shows three labeled chapters.
- **Symptom-flare epochs**: On a chronic-condition thread, mark the first day of each flare as a section. You can then find each flare in the timeline.

## 💼 Work and projects

- **Project log**: Make a thread for each project and add a bead for each milestone. Use linked threads to refer to related projects.
- **Personal standup**: Write a daily entry: the work done, the work planned, and the blocked work. Search the body for "blocked" later. A daily recurring reminder at standup time keeps the cadence.
- **Sprint standup, time-boxed**: Set a recurring reminder for each weekday with a limit equal to the sprint length (for example, 10 occurrences for two weeks). The series deactivates on the last day of the sprint.
- **Meeting notes**: Write an entry for each meeting. Set a comment-reminder for each action item on its due date. The comment-reminder fires when the action is due.
- **Decision journal**: Write an entry for each non-trivial decision, with the decision and the reasons. Set comment-reminders for 1 month and 3 months later that ask "did this work?".
- **Decision → verdict bead**: Promote the answer to the "did this work?" comment-reminder to a bead, and chain it with the decision. The chain then reads *call → verdict → next call*.
- **1:1 prep**: Make a thread for each direct report. Before each 1:1, write an entry with the subjects that you want to discuss.
- **Bug / issue log**: Make a thread for each project. Write an entry for each bug with the steps to reproduce it. Use the bead mood for the severity.
- **Job application tracker**: Make a thread for each role that you apply for. Set comment-reminders for the follow-up dates.
- **Career milestones**: Keep a thread of important work moments. Read it one year later when you update the résumé.
- **Phase-marked project thread**: On a project thread, mark section beads "Kickoff / Build / Launch / Postmortem". The chain then shows four phases, and you do not change the entry titles.
- **Career chapters**: On a career thread, mark sections "Role A / Role B / Sabbatical / Role C". The chain becomes your CV in beads.

## 📚 Learning

- **Course notes**: Make a thread for each course and add a bead for each session. Put comments on the thread for the themes that you find.
- **Spaced repetition**: When you write an important idea, attach comment-reminders for 3 days, 1 week and 1 month later. Each comment-reminder tells you to read the idea again.
- **Book notes**: Make a thread for each book and add a bead for each chapter or session. Use comments for later thoughts, so that you do not rewrite the original notes.
- **Article archive**: Write an entry for each article that you want to keep, with the main point in the body. Link the entry to the related project threads.
- **Skill acquisition**: Make a thread for each skill ("Drawing", "Guitar") and add a bead for each session. Use the mood for an honest self-assessment.
- **Question log**: Write each question that you think of, and search the questions later. Use comment-reminders to go back to unanswered questions in 30 days.
- **Question that found its answer**: When the comment-reminder of an old question fires and you know the answer, write the answer as a comment. Promote and chain the comment, and the beads show *question → answer*.
- **Course modules as sections**: On a course thread, mark the first session of each module as a section. The chain then shows the syllabus.
- **Book parts as sections**: On a book-notes thread, mark the first bead of each part as a section. The chain then agrees with the table of contents.

## 💞 Relationships

- **Person thread**: Make one thread for each important person. Write an entry for each significant interaction, and use the bead mood for the quality of the meeting.
- **Conversation log**: Write a quick entry after an important conversation, with the words in the body. Add a comment later if your opinion of the conversation changes.
- **Birthdays and anniversaries**: Set a yearly recurring reminder with no limit for each person. Write an entry on the thread of that person each time the reminder fires. The chain becomes a birthday letter that grows each year.
- **Letters never sent**: Write an entry addressed to a person, who does not read it. The entry keeps its true date. Add comments later with new thoughts.
- **Family observations**: Make a thread for each child or parent. It becomes a record of small moments that most journals do not keep.
- **Conflict and repair**: Write an entry after a conflict. Set a comment-reminder for one week later that asks "is this still hot?".
- **Relationship chapters**: On a person-thread, mark section beads "Met / Dating / Moved-in / Married". The chain becomes the table of contents of the relationship.

## 🔁 Closing the loop: cycles, not infinite chains

- **Close the year**: On Dec 31, use *Close the loop* on the "2026" thread. The thread closes into a ring and does not grow. The year is a completed cycle, not an open chain.
- **40-day practice**: Make a devotional thread with a fixed length and set the clasp date to day 40. The closed ring shows that the practice is complete. A counter is not necessary.
- **Annual return**: Close a cycle. Then set a comment-reminder on its last bead for one year later. The closed loop then tells you to go through it again.
- **Mourning cycle**: Close a grief thread when the season ends, and reopen it in a year when you need it. A closed loop is complete for now. It is not deleted.
- **Reopen on relapse**: Close a "90 days" thread at the clasp. After a relapse, use *Reopen*, because cycles recur. The clasp date moves and the chain stays.
- **Backdate the clasp**: If you close a thread today but the cycle ended last month, tap the clasp and select the correct date.
- **Auto-month as a clock face**: Open the auto-thread of last month. It is a loop, because a month is a closed cycle, and the 1st is at the clasp. Read the days around the ring.
- **Bibliography of cycles**: Let a new thread mention a closed loop ("descends from «2025 sabbatical»"). You can then cite completed loops as the source of your open work.
- **Read the citations from the loop itself**: Open the closed cycle and tap its `@` chip. The *Mentioned by* list is the bibliography of that cycle. You do not search for the entries that point to it.
- **Walk the closed circuit**: Export a closed thread as slides. The deck ends where it starts, because the loop defines where it closes.
- **Close the small loops too**: A comment frequently records an experiment. *Resolve* strikes the comment through, as *Close the loop* closes a thread. Thus you can open and close items at the level of the comment too.
- **The living trying-list**: Write one comment for each experiment on an entry or a thread. The comments that are not struck are your open list. The struck comments are the closed loops. A checklist app is not necessary.

## ✈️ Travel and place

- **Trip journal**: Make a thread for each trip and add a bead for each day or each stop. Put photos and voice notes in each entry.
- **Place log**: Make a thread for each city or region that you visit again and again. Add a bead for each visit, with a glyph for the year.
- **Restaurant log**: Write an entry for each meal that you want to remember, with photos. Use the bead mood for your opinion of the meal.
- **Hike / outdoor log**: Write an entry for each outing. Add a voice note for the trail conditions and photos of the view.
- **Living somewhere new**: Make a thread for the year of arrival. The day auto-thread records the details of each day, and a collection groups related observations.
- **Trip by legs as chapters**: On a travel thread, mark the first day in each city or country as a section. The bead chain then shows the itinerary.
- **Places I've lived**: On a `Places I've lived` thread, mark the first entry of each home as a section. The chain shows the places in chapters.

## 🎨 Creative practice

- **Idea bank**: Use one thread for all incomplete ideas, where each bead is a fragment. Use search to find a fragment when you need it.
- **Studio log**: Make a thread for each body of work and write an entry for each studio day. Use the bead mood for the energy in the room.
- **Inspiration board**: Write an entry for each source of inspiration (article, image, conversation). Link the threads that the source relates to.
- **Drawing / sketch prompts**: Write an entry for each prompt and attach the photo of the result. A thread chains the practice.
- **Songwriting / poetry seeds**: Write an entry for each phrase or hook that you think of. Use comment-reminders to read the good ones again one month later.
- **Creative-cycle journal**: Record your moods at the start, the middle and the end of a piece. The shape of the chain after some months gives you information.
- **Creative-project arc**: On a creative-project thread, mark sections "Concept / Draft / Revision / Publish". The chain shows the current stage of the work.
- **Verse in a fence**: Put a haiku or part of a poem in a fenced code block. The monospace format stays in the editor read-view, in thread previews, and in *Editorial* / *Journal* exports.

## 🕯 Ritual and contemplative

- **Tarot pull**: Write an entry for each pull, with a photo of the card and a bead glyph for the card name. The day auto-thread records the pull of the day.
- **Astrology reflection**: Put comments on a yearly thread when a transit has an effect that you feel.
- **I Ching consultation**: Write an entry for each reading. Set a comment-reminder for the applicable time ("revisit in 3 weeks").
- **Prayer / spiritual practice**: Write an entry for each session and use the same bead for the practice, because the chain is the important part. Use comments for a text or an idea.
- **Dawn and dusk sit**: Give the morning sit and the evening sit their time-of-day. The day auto-thread puts them in order from dawn to dusk, and not in the order in which you wrote them.
- **Sabbath / day of rest**: Make a thread for each year of weekly rest. Add a bead for each Saturday or Sunday.
- **Liturgical year by season**: On a yearly faith thread, mark section beads "Advent / Christmas / Lent / Easter". The chain follows the church year.
- **The bell and the dice**: A comment-reminder or a recurring reminder brings back an old entry because you set it. Wander on the Home header (opt-in) opens a random entry older than your threshold. Thus a contemplative re-read can be intentional or random.

## ⏳ Time, goals, and reviews

- **365-day challenge**: Anchor a thread to the year and add one bead each day. The challenge is to keep the chain complete. A daily recurring reminder with a limit of 365 keeps the cadence and deactivates itself on day 365.
- **Goal milestones**: Make a thread for each goal and add a bead for each milestone. Set a comment-reminder on the deadline that asks "what's the status?".
- **A day you can walk in order**: Give each entry the time-of-day when it occurred. The day auto-thread then shows the entries in time order (dawn entry → noon → night), and not in the order in which you wrote them.
- **Quarterly review**: Set a recurring reminder every 3 months. Set a limit of 4 occurrences for a review cycle of one year, or set no limit for a permanent cadence.
- **Annual reflection**: Set a yearly recurring reminder with no limit. Use it with a `New Year` thread, and the chain gets one bead each January.
- **Future-self letter**: Write an entry addressed to a date far in the future. Set a comment-reminder that fires on that date and tells you to read the entry.
- **Lessons learned**: Write an entry for each lesson. Set a comment-reminder for 6 months later to examine if the lesson is still true.
- **Habit retirement**: When you stop a daily recurring reminder that you used for months, **Stop** keeps the counter. The number of occurrences (for example, "247 fired") is the record of how long the practice continued.
- **Era markers on a life-thread**: On a long life-thread, mark sections "Before / After" at the bead of an important event. The chain shows the change, and you do not rewrite the earlier entries.
- **A date you can walk back to**: Anchor a thread (a vow, a person, the year) to the day it started. That calendar day keeps a ribbon. The day opens the thread directly, also when the day has no entry.
- **Step out of the chain**: Tap the **bead-count chip** on a long thread (a project, a person, the year). Its beads become notebook cards on Home, with the thread name pinned above the search bar. *"search within this thread…"* searches in the thread, and × on the pill removes the thread scope.
- **On this day, in beads**: Open Home on the same date one year after an important entry. An `ON THIS DAY · PRIOR YEARS` band shows above TODAY, with the beads of the same date in each earlier year.
- **The quiet thread**: A per-practice thread with no new bead for half a year moves to the `QUIET · 180d+` tail of the threads list. This is a third state between a live thread and a closed thread, and you do not have to close or delete the thread. Add a bead, and the thread goes back to TODAY. Use *Close the loop*, and the thread leaves Quiet for the closed state.

## 🧩 Niche and special-purpose

- **Garden / plant journal**: Make a thread for each plant or bed and write an entry for each intervention. Use comments for the result months later. Set a recurring reminder for each plant for its watering cadence (every 7 days for a fern, every 3 days for a basil).
- **Pet observations**: Make a thread for each pet. Write an entry for vet visits, behavior changes and milestones.
- **Home log**: Make a thread for the house, with repairs, problems and project ideas. Add a comment when you repair the problem that you recorded six months before.
- **Travel back from a hard time**: Make a thread for the recovery period, apart from the daily entries. Later, the chain is the record of the recovery.
- **Seasonal observation**: Make one thread for each season of each year. The entries record the weather, the garden and the mood.
- **House project chapters**: On a renovation thread, mark sections "Demo / Reno / Finish / Decor". The chain shows the stages of the project.
- **Pet biography**: On a pet thread, mark sections "Puppy / Adult / Senior". The chain shows the life of the pet in three labeled chapters.
- **Recipe iterations**: On a dish thread, mark sections "First attempt / Refined / Hosted-it". The section boundaries show when the recipe became stable.

## 🔁 Recurring nudges

A recurring reminder is one row that advances. It always points at the next occurrence. When you acknowledge it, the cursor advances. One step catches up all missed occurrences ("*N due since you last looked*"). The entries below show ways to use that pattern.

### 🌅 Daily

- **Evening review**: Every day at 21:00, with no limit. Write three lines. When you acknowledge the reminder, it advances to tomorrow.
- **Morning intention**: Every day at the start of your routine. The body of the entry is the one priority of the day.
- **Gratitude practice**: Every day, with no limit. Write an entry on a `Gratitude` thread each time the reminder fires.
- **Wakeful pause**: Every day at midday. The body has no prescribed content. This is a 30-second checkpoint.
- **Medication / supplement**: Every day at intake time, with no limit. A one-line entry confirms the intake.
- **Hydration check**: Every day in the middle of the afternoon. Use the bead mood to record how the body feels.
- **One-sentence diary**: Every day at bedtime, with no limit. Write one sentence on a year thread.

### 📅 Weekly / fortnightly

- **Weekly review**: Every 1 week on Sunday. Link the entry to the projects thread, and use comments for the unexpected events of the week.
- **Sabbath / day of rest**: Every 1 week. The chain of entries becomes the sabbath thread of the year.
- **Friend check-in rotation**: Every 1 week. The body names the person that you contacted. The thread of each person grows with time.
- **Bi-weekly retro**: Every 2 weeks. Write an entry of two paragraphs: the things that worked and the things to stop.
- **Plant care: succulents**: Every 1 week. Use a glyph for the plant ("J" for jade).
- **Plant care: monstera**: Every 3 days. Each plant can have its own cadence.
- **Cleanout day**: Every 2 weeks. You select the inbox, the closet or the desktop.

### 🗓 Monthly / quarterly / yearly

- **Bills check**: Every month, with no limit. The comments on the reminder collect context during the year.
- **Period tracker prompt**: Every 28 days. The body records the condition of the cycle.
- **Quarterly review**: Every 3 months, with a limit of 4 occurrences for one year. Pin a thread for each quarter and add a bead each time.
- **Annual reflection**: Every year, with no limit. Each occurrence is an entry on a `New Year` thread, which becomes longer each January.
- **Birthday letter**: Every year on the date, with no limit. Write an entry on a per-person thread each time the reminder fires. After decades, the chain is a long letter.
- **Tax prep checkpoints**: Every 3 months, with a limit of 4 occurrences.
- **Anniversary memo**: Every year, with or without a limit. Use it to read the entry of the same date last year.

### 🎯 Bounded / programmatic

- **30-day challenge**: Every day, with a limit of 30 occurrences. The series deactivates when you acknowledge the 30th occurrence.
- **21-day kickstart**: Every day, with a limit of 21 occurrences. This is the same pattern with a shorter duration.
- **90-day practice**: Every day, with a limit of 90 occurrences. Use it with a thread to see the 90 beads accumulate.
- **Pet medication**: Every day, with a limit equal to the prescription duration. Acknowledge each dose.
- **Therapy homework**: Every week, with a limit equal to the number of remaining sessions. A series with a limit ends by itself.
- **Project standup for a sprint**: Every weekday for 10 weekdays (a limit of 10 occurrences). Use this when a habit is temporary.

### ✨ Emergent patterns

- **Honest catchup**: When you miss occurrences of a recurring reminder, the app shows "*N due since you last looked*" and not N banners. One acknowledgment moves the cursor after all of them.
- **Stop without losing the cursor**: Long-press → Stop keeps `recurrence_fired` and the next occurrence. When you resume, the series continues from the same point. Use this for a vacation or a retreat.
- **Edit without resetting**: When you edit the rule (every 1 week → every 2 weeks), the fired counter does not change. The series counts the occurrences that fired, not those that the new rule gives.
- **The reminder as accumulator**: A recurring reminder that continues for a long time ("Pay rent") collects comments during the year. The reminder row becomes a small yearly record. Tap its 💬 chip in the calendar panel to read the history.
- **Recurring + thread = chain by cadence**: Write an entry for each occurrence of a recurring reminder, and add the bead of that entry to a thread. The thread then shows the cadence of the practice.

## 🪡 Patterns that combine features

- **The same thread in two collections**: A "Marathon training 2026" thread can be in the `Habits` collection and in the `Projects` collection. You do not have to select one collection.
- **Name a six-month pile**: In select mode, put many loose threads into one named collection in one operation.
- **Sweep away the done**: Delete a group of completed threads in one multi-select operation. The operation skips auto-threads, so you can select mixed kinds.
- **A throwaway thread to read by**: Put several saved beads on one temporary thread, read the combined chain, and then delete the thread. The thread is the method to show these bead kinds together.
- **Auto-thread as a day-summary read**: Open the auto-thread of today to see the day as beads. You write nothing new.
- **Hour-stamped day onto the Emacs agenda**: Give an entry its time-of-day. The Org export writes `[2026-06-08 Mon 14:30]`, so the entry shows in your `org-agenda` at the correct hour.
- **Comments on reminders**: A recurring "Pay rent" reminder collects comments during the year (where, how, what was different). The reminder becomes a small yearly record.
- **Self-ping ladder**: When you record a hard moment, attach three comment-reminders: 1 day, 1 week, 1 month. At each comment-reminder, you can add a comment about how the moment changed.
- **Search comments for old half-baked ideas**: The home search includes comment bodies. You can find the "I'll come back to this" notes that you wrote in comments years before.
- **Linked threads as soft tags**: To make an entry refer to a thread with no bead in that thread, link the thread and do not add the bead. This is a light cross-reference.
- **Read the mentions backwards**: The `@` chip of a thread lists each entry that mentions the thread. Thus a mention that points out of an entry is also readable from the thread, with no more work.
- **Cadence as a bead, not a thread**: A daily dream journal is 365 beads of the same kind, which is a vocabulary and not a story. Give each dream a saved *Dream* bead, and find the full year from the search-bar typeahead. Use a thread when each bead is a change of direction. The same pattern works for a per-meal symptom log, a weekly check-in, or a daily standup.
- **A thread of threads (informal)**: Make a `Meta` collection that groups your most-used threads (year log, gratitude, decisions). The collection is the index.
- **Namespace by name**: Give user threads a prefix: `Person · …`, `Proj · …`, `Q2 · …`. Set the threads list to A–Z, and the prefixes group the threads into sub-sections in their collections. Collections cannot give this sub-level, and a folder tree is not necessary.
- **Numbered collections**: The collection selector lists collections alphabetically. If you name them `1 · Now`, `2 · Soon`, `3 · Someday`, the picker shows them in priority order and not in creation order.
- **Ad-hoc cross-collection lens**: Select `Habits` + `Reflection` + `Travel` together in the selector for one combined review, then clear the selection. This is a temporary union and not a merge. The collections stay separate, and each thread keeps its memberships.
- **A hub entry of Mentions**: Write one entry that mentions your ten most important threads, with text between the cards. Unlike the `Meta` collection, this index can explain why each thread is there.
- **The hub, read from each spoke**: After a hub entry mentions ten threads, each of those threads has an `@` chip that goes back to the hub. You can get to the index from each thread that it points at.
- **Recurring reminder + comments + thread**: The recurring reminder sets the cadence. You add the bead of each entry to a per-practice thread, and you record thoughts as comments on that entry. Months later, the thread shows the practice and the comments give the details.
- **Retreat mode**: Stop your recurring reminders before a week offline and resume the same cadence after. The counter and the cursor stay, and no old banners show.
- **Comments as seedbed**: Each comment is a decision for later. It can stay a comment, get a self-ping ladder, or become a bead.
- **Highlight ⇄ promote feedback loop**: A comment-reminder fires on a starred entry. Promote the answer to a bead and give it the weekly ★. The auto-thread of the week then contains an entry that started as a comment.
- **Section + mention as cross-reference**: Mark a bead as a section, and let its entry mention another thread. The chapter title is then also a link to the related thread (the reading list of a season, the project plan of a year).
- **Section + monthly star**: Mark a bead as a section, and give its entry the monthly star. The chapter break then shows in the thread and in the month auto-thread.
- **Heading-as-landmark + section-as-bead**: Start an entry with `# Decision`, then mark its bead as a section on the parent thread. The change then has a name in the entry body and a chapter tag on the chain.
- **Quote your past self**: Paste a blockquote of an entry of last month at the top of the entry of today. The `> ` block is a cross-reference in the body, without a mention.
- **Saved bead → lens → chain**: Make a "decision" bead and save it. Weeks later, filter the list by that saved bead and use *Select all → thread*. All your decisions go into one thread.
- **Meaning-free shape as cover**: The app does not name shapes, so only you know the meaning of your decagon. You can read the journal, but a person who looks at your screen cannot.
- **Long-press the bead, summon its kin**: Long-press the bead in an entry row on Home. If the four facets match a saved bead, its pill appears above the search bar with its name and live count. If they do not match, the funnel comes on with that exact `{shape,pattern,mood,glyph}` set. You do not type a preset prefix or open the Filters sheet.
- **Echo at the moment of writing**: When you compose a bead, the meta strip of the editor shows a `⤺ {date}` chip. The chip links to the previous entry with that bead. Tap the chip, and the chip in that entry points one step further back. Thus you can go back through each earlier use of the bead.
- **Count your beads at the threshold**: The substrate phrase in the Home header shows its count (`15 Beads of life` / `5 Threads of life`). A pinned thread or bead scope narrows the count to that practice. You can read the count before you add the bead of today. When the count is zero, the header shows no number.

## 🔖 Sections: chapters within a thread

A user thread can have **chapter headers**. When you name a bead, it becomes the start of a named section in the chain. In the detail view, the section name shows as a price-tag that a thin string ties to the chain. In compact previews (threads list, batch picker, the inline thread chains of EntryCard), the bead has a soft gold ring. A section belongs to a membership: an entry in two threads can be a chapter header in one thread and an ordinary bead in the other. A section marker stays with its bead. If you move the bead, the chapter moves. If you remove the bead, the chapter becomes part of the previous chapter.

- **Year as chapters**: On a thread anchored to the year, mark the first bead of each month as a section. In December, the chain is a book of 12 chapters, and you do not write an annual summary.
- **The inflection bead**: Mark as a section the one bead of an important change (a decision, a breakup, a diagnosis). The chain then reads "before / after", and the change has a name.
- **Mid-chain chapter reset**: When a thread is months old and its direction changes, mark the first bead of the new direction as section "Chapter 2". The chain shows the change, and you do not start a new thread.
- **Promote-into-a-section**: Promote a comment to a bead, chain it, then mark it as a section header. The comment that became important then has a chapter title.
- **Anniversary chapter**: A yearly recurring reminder gives one entry each year on a person-thread. Mark the first bead of each year as a section. The chain then reads as a letter with one chapter for each year.
- **Same bead, two chapters**: The same entry can be a "Kickoff" header on one thread and a plain bead on another thread. A section belongs to the membership and not to the entry, so one moment can have different meanings in different threads.
- **Therapy themes over years**: On a therapy thread that continues for years, mark the main theme of each year as a section. The chain shows the work theme by theme.
- **Sabbatical phases**: On a sabbatical thread, mark section beads "Pre / Away / Re-entry". The journal then has the three parts of the time away.
- **Children's developmental stages**: On a kid-observation thread, mark sections "Infant / Toddler / Preschool / School". The chain reads as the biography of the child in chapters.
- **Floating chapter**: Mark a bead as a section in a thread that you do not fully understand yet. The gold ring and the tag show that the bead is important before you can say why.
- **Editable mirror of an auto-thread**: Auto-week and auto-month threads are derived, and you cannot mark sections on them. Make a user year-thread and mark the month boundaries as sections to get a version that you can curate.

## ⚗️ Cleaning up bursts (fuse & thread)

Quick captures accumulate fast: photos in the same place, voice memos one after the other. Fuse or Thread cleans the group, and you keep all that you captured.

- **Let the journal notice**: The app offers a cleanup for a burst of quick captures. You do not have to remember to do it.
- **One moment, many shots → Fuse**: Many photos of one scene become one entry. Its bead is the *alchemy* of the contributors: the shape and the mood that they had in common.
- **A sequence of moments → Thread**: Keep each capture complete and put the beads into a chain. The walk that you photographed becomes a thread.
- **Tidy an old pile**: The cleanup is not only for the last half-hour. You can fuse the loose photos of last week in the same way.

## ✍️ Publishing what you wrote

The article composer changes a thread into a post that you can share as Markdown, self-contained HTML or PDF. You do not write the content again. The entries stay the source of truth. The composition (title, intro, template, hidden sections, overrides) is stored on the thread. Some ways to use it:

- **Trip recap**: Use a travel thread with one paragraph of intro for each stop. Export as HTML and send it to friends. The bead chain at the top identifies the trip.
- **Year-in-review**: Use a thread of milestones anchored to the year. Compose with the *Editorial* template, share as PDF, and print a copy for yourself.
- **Project retrospective**: Use a thread that contains the beads of a project. Hide the daily entries, keep the important changes, and write an intro of one paragraph for each section. Export as Markdown and paste it into the wiki or blog of your team.
- **Newsletter from a daily auto-thread**: Select the auto-thread of an important day, write a short intro, and export as Markdown. Paste it into Substack. You do not rewrite the day.
- **Practice log → public post**: Use a meditation, running or writing-practice thread with the *Journal* template (cream + Caveat title). Export as HTML to get a file that you can share.
- **Gift it as a PDF**: Complete a parenting, friendship or collaboration thread. Compose it, write a personal intro, and export as PDF. The chain is at the top and the entries are below. You can give the file as a gift.
- **Markdown roundtrip into Obsidian / Hugo / Jekyll**: The Markdown export is a self-contained folder: `<slug>.md` + `assets/`. The assets are the photos, the voice notes, and the bead `chain.svg` as the header of the note. Unzip it into a vault or a static-site repo. Each image, each recording and the chain resolve with no changes.
- **A thread becomes a roam-able note**: Put the unzipped folder into an Obsidian vault. The bead chain is the note banner, and the mentions show as a "see also" footer. The thread is then part of your graph and keeps its bead chain.
- **One bead, one note (the thread as a map-of-content)**: Set *One file per bead*. The export is then an `index.md` MOC that links a folder of per-bead notes. Each note is a node that can have backlinks, so the chain becomes a Zettelkasten in one export.
- **Hand someone their own folder**: Export a person-thread to Markdown and give the unzipped folder to that person. It contains their photos, their voice notes and their chain. No app and no account are necessary.

- **Trip recap, your reactions included**: Show friends the trip and your comment on each stop, in a form that they can read as a social post.
- **Wedding / birthday / milestone keepsake**: Make a scrapbook that a person can keep. You can print it, give it or mail it.

## 🌉 A second life in Emacs (Org export)

A thread exports as a `.zip` for use in Emacs. The export maps the data to the Org grammar. Auto-threads become a native datetree. Mentions become org-roam links. The bead facets become a drawer that you can query, with an inline SVG. The export is one-way and final. Give the zip to the OS, and continue the journal in Emacs.

- **org-datetree for free**: A day, week or month auto-thread exports as a native `* 2026 / ** 2026-05 / *** 2026-05-17` tree. Years of journal then work with `org-agenda` and datetree navigation, with no changes.
- **The roam graph absorbs the journal**: Each entry has a generated `:ID:`, and each mention becomes an `[[id:]]` link. Your thread cross-references go into an org-roam graph as nodes.
- **Query the bead grammar with org-ql**: Shape, mood, pattern and glyph export as a `:PROPERTIES:` drawer. Thus "every grief-flower bead in 2026" is an `org-ql` query.
- **The bead survives the plain-text trip**: The inline SVG of each bead keeps the bead image in a raw Org buffer. Emacs cannot draw the bead by itself.
- **The self-ping ladder reads inline**: The comment-reminder dialogue of an entry exports as the commentary of the node. The comments show below the heading of the entry in Emacs.
- **DONE in the margins**: A resolved comment exports as a native org `DONE` headline. Open comments have no keyword, by design. Thus `org-agenda`/`org-ql` show each closed comment, and the journal does not become a task list.
- **Recurring reminder as the datetree spine**: A daily or weekly recurring reminder sets the cadence. Its acknowledged entries export into the datetree as the dated structure of an org-journal.
- **A journal that lives in git**: Reproducible mode omits the export timestamp/version. A second export of the same thread then gives a clean diff, and you can keep the journal in a tracked repo.
- **Locked entries stay locked across the bridge**: The export writes locked entries as masked stubs or `:crypt:`-tagged nodes. A thread can go into Emacs, and your private entries do not become plain text.
- **vakana.el on tap**: The zip contains an optional helper. Put it into your config, and Emacs renders the beads and reads the properties. You write no elisp.
- **Fuse, then bridge**: Fuse a capture burst into one entry first. Then the fused bead, and not the burst, goes into your agenda and roam graph.

## 📥 The desktop hands it back (note import)

This is the opposite of the Org export. The app imports a plain-text note, written on another device, as an uncurated draft. You capture on the desktop and curate on the phone. The org-datetree mapping operates in the opposite direction. The app does not sync or merge. No data leaves your devices unless you configure a location.

- **org-capture all week, curate Sunday**: Capture into one Org datetree from Emacs during the week. On the phone, the import gives one week of dated drafts. Give them beads and chain them in one session.
- **The bridge runs both ways**: Export a thread to Org, use it on the desktop, and append more days to its datetree. Vakana then imports the new days.
- **Capture cheap, encode deliberate**: Capture on the desktop is fast. You still select the bead on the phone, so the import does not select a bead automatically.
- **A draft lands on its real day**: The date ladder puts an imported note on the day you wrote it, not on the day it synced. The calendar and the day auto-thread stay correct.
- **Syncthing as the silent courier**: Point Vakana at a folder that syncs peer-to-peer. Notes appear with no cloud, no account and no server. The data stays local-only across your devices.
- **A throwaway URL, a quick graft**: Paste a gist-raw link or a LAN link to import a snippet from another tool into today as a draft. Then put it on the applicable thread.
- **Then weave it in**: An imported draft has no connections until you add a **mention**. The mention attaches the draft to the thread that it answers.
- **Fuse what the desktop dumped**: Import a capture file with many small notes, then **fuse & thread** it into one fused bead. This is the same cleanup that you use for bursts on the phone.

## 🎞 Walk the thread: the deck

Export a thread as a `.zip` that opens as a deck. The deck has one station for each bead, and you click the chain to move through it. The deck is a memory palace route and not a set of slides. The export is one-way and final. The viewer, not you, selects light or dark.

- **Walk it live, no export**: Open the entry of a bead from a thread and tap **Next** to go through the chain in the editor. The `n / N` shows your position, and Back goes to the chain. You do not build a deck.
- **Walk someone your year**: Export an `auto_month` thread. It opens as a dated route, and the starred entries are the chapter breaks. You do not write a summary.
- **The talk only you can hear**: Your comment-reminder ladder exports as speaker notes that only the presenter sees. The audience sees the entry, and you see your later comments.
- **Click the bead, walk to the station**: The chain at the bottom of each slide is the table of contents and the map of the route. Tap a bead to go directly to its station.
- **A person-thread, presented**: Give a person an `index.html` of their thread. The bead chain is the structure, and the dominant mood is the color of the deck.
- **Chapters become acts**: Section beads export as full-bleed divider slides, with a gold ring on the chain. A year-thread opens with the chapters that you marked.
- **Their light, their dark**: The deck contains the two cappuccino palettes and remembers the selection of the reader. You do not select a palette for the reader.

## 🧵 Spotting the woven entries

- **Find floating moments to chain**: Look in the list for rows without a thread chip. Put the important ones on a thread.
- **One tap to the drift**: Open Filters → flip *Unwoven only*. The list shows each entry with no thread and no mention, and you can read or chain them. The app shows these entries only when you ask, never as a banner.
- **Find and weave in one motion**: With *Unwoven only* on, *Select all N → thread* puts all those entries on one thread (a `Loose ideas`, an `Inbox-of-fragments`) in one operation. The filter is the query, and the footer does the action.
- **Loosen the cut**: Use *Unwoven only* together with a saved bead. The list then shows only the unwoven entries with that bead (each unwoven *deep work* bead, each unwoven *hard talk*).
- **Read the orbit, not the chain**: Flip *Mentions a thread*. The list shows each entry that mentions a thread but is on no thread. You can read your cross-references as one group.
- **The mentions that never became beats**: Use *Mentions a thread* together with a saved bead. The list shows the entries where you mentioned a practice with that bead but did not chain them. Each one is a candidate for the chain that it mentions.
- **Reach what you never typed**: *Has a voice note* is the only way to find the entries that you spoke and did not write. Search reads the title, the body and the comments, and a recording has none of these.
- **The wordless week**: Use *Has a photo* on a week with few written entries. It shows the days that you recorded in images only. Give the weekly star to the good ones, and the auto-week thread chains them.
- **Voice notes, filed at last**: Use *Has a voice note* together with *Unwoven only* to find each recording that is on no thread. *Select all N → thread* puts them all on a `Spoken drafts` chain in one operation.
- **Walk in from the citations**: The `@` list of a thread shows each entry that mentions the thread but is not in its chain. Read it to find the related notes (the meta-notes, the "this reminded me of…").
- **Re-read your inflection points**: The entries on 2 or more threads are the moments that were important in more than one thread.
- **Monthly cleanup pass**: Review the unwoven entries when a recurring reminder fires. For each entry, decide: chain it, comment on it, or leave it.
- **Promote the comment that wouldn't fade**: If a comment-reminder fired two times on the same comment, the comment is possibly important enough to be a bead. Promote it, and the reminders for it stop.
- **Scan chains for chapter beads**: Soft gold rings in preview chains show the section-header beads. Use them to find your position in long threads in the threads list or the batch picker.
- **Find the talked-about beads**: A small dot on a bead in the chain of a thread shows that its entry has comments. Look along the chain to find the entries with comments. Tap the bead to open those comments.

## 👁 Peek: keep another note open while you write

The **eye** in the editor opens another note read-only adjacent to your draft, as a side buffer does in Emacs. You do not leave the draft. The draft does not unmount. A note that you opened goes back to the top below *Recently peeked*, so you can open a reference with one tap.

- **Carry the bead forward**: Peek the same-bead note that your Echo chip points to. Write the entry of today in your own words, with the older entry open adjacent to it.
- **A living index**: Keep one note that lists your people, projects or open questions. Peek it while you write, and it stays below *Recently peeked*. It is a private table of contents, one tap from each draft.
- **Consult the earlier station**: In the middle of a practice, peek the last entry of the thread. Then write the new entry as an answer to the old one.
- **Two languages, one desk**: Draft the French entry with its Malagasy version peeked open adjacent to it, or the opposite. Write in one language while you read the other.
- **Did it hold?**: Peek the decision that you made last spring while you write about its result. The decision and the result are on the same page.

## 🌱 From comment to bead: when a margin note grows up

When you go back to a comment again and again, promote it to a bead. The bead can be standalone, or chained to the entry or thread where you wrote the comment. The promotion removes the original comment, so that one thought is in one place.

- **The comment that wouldn't stop**: If you frequently want to reply to a comment, promote it to a bead chained with its parent. Do not add one more reply in the drawer.
- **Sunday graduation**: On Sunday evening, promote old comment-reminders that became important. Give them the ★ so that they go into the current `Week of …` auto-thread.
- **From self-ping ladder to one piece**: When the 1d/1w/1m comment-reminder dialogue gives one idea, promote that idea to a bead. The other comments of the ladder become the commentary of that bead in *Storyboard*.
- **Retroactive bead**: Promote today an old comment that became true. The bead has the date of today, and the body keeps the text that you wrote then.
- **Two-bead spawn into a deliberate thread**: Promote-and-chain on an entry puts the two beads in `ThreadComposer`, with the name prefilled as `(From:) <entry title>`. The thread starts from a comment.
- **Promote-into-the-week**: Promote a comment to a bead and give it the weekly ★. It goes on the current `Week of …` auto-thread, with the other important entries of the week.
- **Habit drift detection**: On a habit thread, promote the friction comments ("skipped, tired") to chained beads. The chain then shows the periods of doubt.
- **Cross-day realization**: A comment on a Monday entry that you promote on Wednesday goes on the auto-day thread of Wednesday. The bead belongs to the day of the promotion, not to the day of the comment.
- **Person-thread distillation**: Promote one idea from a year of comments on a person-thread to a bead on a `Realizations` thread. The bead keeps the person-thread as a mention.
- **Promote then Card**: Promote a comment to a bead, give it the monthly ★, and export it as a *Card with Notes* PNG. The notes on the card are the comment-reminders that fired after the promotion.
- **Promote then chapter-mark**: Promote a drawer comment to a bead, chain it to its parent thread, then mark it as a section header. The comment becomes a bead and then a chapter title.

- **Thread coda**: Write the meta-entry that ends a long thread and export it as *Editorial*. The summary reads as an article without the chain.
- **Auto-thread highlight**: Select one entry from the auto-thread of today and export it as *Postcard*. The day becomes one file that you can share.
- **Self-ping conversation**: A comment-reminder ladder (1 day / 1 week / 1 month) can add follow-up comments to an entry. *Storyboard* exports the entry and those comments as one piece, in the form of a Twitter thread.
- **Decision + revisit**: Use a decision entry whose comment-reminders gave "did this work?" comments months later. *Storyboard* shows the decision and its result together.
- **Year-in-one-entry**: Write the yearly retrospective on the anchored thread of the year and export it as *Editorial*. The article has the chain banner of your year.
- **Person-thread excerpt**: On a person-thread, select the entry that says what you want the person to hear. Export it as *Journal* HTML and send it.

- **Day-bead made public**: When one bead records the full day (mood color, glyph for the main event), export the entry as *Storyboard*. The bead avatar at the top of the post identifies the day.

- **Bead-of-the-day badge**: Export a daily mood-log entry as a *Card*. The bead avatar is the image of the day, in a format that is applicable to social stories.
- **Practice badge**: Export an entry on a meditation, running or writing thread as a *Card*. It shows the entry of today on the chain, with your private bead as identity.

- **Fuse-then-Card**: Fuse a burst of 5 photos into one entry, then export a *Card* PNG. You share one image and not 5 thumbnails.
- **Birthday card you actually wrote**: A yearly recurring reminder on a person-thread collects entries. A *Card with Notes* that includes the comments of past years becomes the birthday gift of that year.

## 🔣 Your own bead language: saved beads

- **Build your own type system**: The app gives no meaning to shapes, so a circle can be your "deep work" and a flower your "grief". Save each as a named bead. The vocabulary is then yours and not that of the app.
- **Ritual bead, one tap**: Save the morning-prayer bead one time and recall it with one tap each morning. The practice keeps the same bead along a thread for the year, and you do not select it again each day.
- **Composing becomes the rare act**: When the editor opens to your saved beads, you make a new bead only as a deliberate exception. The vocabulary becomes stable.
- **Refactor your symbology**: After six months, you decide that "deep work" must be ember and not slate. Long-press the saved bead and use *Reshape across history*. Each past entry with that bead shows the new bead, and so do the chains that contain it.
- **Split a meaning that drifted**: "exercise" became *run* and *gym*. Reshape the old bead to *run* and rewrite the past, then save *gym* as a new bead for future entries. You do not rewrite the entries manually.
- **Rename without disturbing the past**: Rename "Tana trip" to "Antananarivo 2026", and your history does not change. The entries keep the look of the bead and not its name.
- **Encode ⇄ retrieve symmetry**: A saved bead marks "long run" on the entry of today. In the Filters sheet, the same saved bead is the filter that finds all long runs.
- **Search the picker by your own bead names**: When you add a bead to a thread, start from the Saved Beads strip of the picker. Tap *morning run*, and the picker shows only the morning-run entries. You search with your own vocabulary.
- **Pin a saved *vakana* above the search bar**: Type `mor`, and a *morning run* chip shows below the box. One tap pins it as a pill above the bar and clears the input, so that you can search in it. The list shows only the morning runs.
- **Search within a pinned bead**: A preset pinned above the search bar limits the input to the entries of that bead. Type `Lisp` to find each *deep work* entry that contains it.
- **Glance at the density**: Pin a saved *vakana*, and the pill shows its live count adjacent to the name. *morning run · 200* is a thread for a full year, and *flash insight · 3* is a bead that you used few times. The count decreases while you type in the text input, so you see how rare a combination is.
- **A preset per person**: Save a bead whose shape and mood mean one person to you. Recall it with one tap when the person appears again, and chain it to their per-person thread.
- **Practiced image, not a label**: A saved bead is a practiced mnemonic image, as in the Memory Palace method. You recall the stored image of "the Tana-trip feeling" and do not make it again each time.
- **Loosen the lens**: Apply a saved bead as a filter, then remove its pattern facet. The filter changes from the exact bead to the full family. The preset starts the search and does not limit it.
- **Query becomes a chapter**: Filter the list by a saved bead and use *Select all → thread*. A practice spread across months becomes one curated chain.
- **Glyph as private cipher**: The glyph is the one place where you name the bead. A saved bead with `☾` lets you filter "night thoughts" by a mark that only you understand.
- **One vocabulary, two people**: Keep the same shape and mood for "hard talk" on the threads of Mom and Dad, but use different glyphs (`M` / `D`). The filter in the Filters sheet finds all hard talks, and the glyph shows the person.

## 🌿 The draft's own chain: edit history

In the editor, the tree icon above the keyboard opens the edit history of this session. Vakana shows the history as a chain of beads and keeps the forks.

- **A rewrite is a fork, not an overwrite**: Undo, then write the sentence differently. The first version stays on its own dashed branch adjacent to the trunk. You can go to the two versions until you leave the note.
- **Compare two openings in place**: Tap one bead, then another, and the note above the sheet changes from one draft to the other. The buffer shows the difference.
- **Rescue from three edits ago**: A paragraph that you cut, and then wrote past, is still a bead on an abandoned branch. Preview it to confirm, then tap again to restore it. Auto-save makes the restore permanent.
- **Walk the writing session the way the echo chip walks the journal**: Go through the session bead by bead. The beads are only the states where you paused, with the newest at the top.
- **Pause, then prune**: Before you leave a long entry, open the tree, look at each branch one time, and restore the state that you want to keep. The other branches go when the session ends, as a *tohivakana* keeps only the beads on its string.
